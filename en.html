In February 2024, the court ruled that:

The judge of first instance correctly deduced from the documents submitted and the arguments conducted before him that, from the outset, Ildikó BÓDI failed to respect the principles of joint parental authority by making decisions on behalf of the child without prior consultation with the father, and that she failed to respect court decisions by moving the domicile and habitual residence of their shared child to Hungary and enrolling her in school there, thereby infringing upon the father's rights by depriving him of the opportunity to spend maximum quality time with the child and by making the exercise of his rights of access and accommodation more costly due to the long journeys required and the accommodation to be arranged in Hungary.

Furthermore, the appellant continued to refuse to comply with the judgment of November 16, 2023, which is provisionally enforceable, by obstinately insisting on keeping the child in Hungary and even going so far as to file a criminal complaint against Filipe FONSECA when he traveled to Hungary to retrieve their shared child while in possession of an enforceable title. The appellant in no way sought dialogue with Filipe FONSECA with a view to finding a solution in the best interests of their shared child, which she claims were not respected by the judgment of November 16, 2023.

This attitude of Ildikó BÓDI is also evident from the telephone exchanges between the parties submitted by Filipe FONSECA, in which Ildikó BÓDI maintains that everywhere in the world a mother's rights with respect to her child must take precedence.

However, it emerges, on the one hand, from the conclusions of the social investigation of January 13, 2022, that both parents have a good relationship with the child and that the child does not refuse contact with her father, whose behavior is appropriate for the child's age, contrary to the claims emerging from the unsubstantiated witness statements produced by Ildikó BÓDI. The fact that the mother is the child's primary reference person is, on the other hand, due to Ildikó BÓDI's violation of the father's rights by excluding him from caring for the child from birth, preferring to rely on her own mother, and ultimately, following the couple's separation, moving geographically away from the father in such a way as to seriously jeopardize the exercise of his rights with respect to their shared child.

In view of all these elements, and more specifically Ildikó BÓDI's inability to respect Filipe FONSECA's rights with respect to their shared child Lily, as well as her inability to respect the latter's right to maintain regular contact with her father—which is nevertheless necessary for her development into a responsible adult—the contested judgment must be upheld insofar as it fixed the legal domicile and habitual residence of their shared child Lily with the father, Filipe FONSECA.

It is also rightly that, in the best interests of their shared child, the judge of first instance established extended rights of access and accommodation in favor of the mother—with whom the child had primarily lived in the past—until Lily's compulsory schooling begins in September 2024, and necessarily more restricted rights of access and accommodation due to schooling from that date onward.

The contested judgment must therefore be upheld on all these points.
